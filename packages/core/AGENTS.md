# Game rules, sector format, and model registry

The instructions are split by package, and a section named in a "see ..." lives in one of these files:

- `AGENTS.md` (repository root): tech stack, package graph, build and test, logging, deployment, lint and format, code conventions, localization.
- `packages/protocol/AGENTS.md`: the wire protocol's framing, the pre-login limit, characters, joining, interest, and what breaks the wire.
- `packages/core/AGENTS.md`: the gameplay rules and their frames (movement, doors, acting, abilities, threat, elites, bosses, gear, trading, drops, speech, pools, raising, fixtures, lucidity, quests, the bounty board), the sector format, and the model registry.
- `packages/web/AGENTS.md`: the browser client, the web map editor, the web asset pack, and hosting.

## Gameplay rules

The rules are shared by the server and the client, and each is given with the frames that carry it. The framing of those frames, and the frames of login, characters, joining, and interest, are in `packages/protocol/AGENTS.md`.

**Movement.** The client predicts and reports: `move {x, z, facing, gait}` about ten times a second, plus a waypoint whenever the straight line from its last report to its next step would be illegal.

The player walks where a press sends them (see "A press on the play field" above), and `GameplayPredictor` takes every step through `resolveMove`. A tap's walk is a path, followed waypoint by waypoint (`PATH_FOLLOWING` in `packages/web/src/client/predictor.ts`). A steer heads straight for the ground under the held pointer, gliding along walls and round bodies, and becomes a path to where the pointer came up. A leg that brings the player no nearer for 0.25 s has its path searched again round the bodies where they stand by then, and a search that finds no way waits 0.25 s and tries again. One destination gets its first search and four more, then the walk ends, so a rare unwalkable leg stops or re-routes the player rather than earning a correction. A new walk replaces the one under way, except that a held press's steer lets a leap end first (see Lunge under "Abilities and effects"). A fall, a correction, an overlay, and Escape end it (Escape leaves a leap running), and so does a lost pointer for a steer, while a path goes on through chat focus, a hidden tab, and the window losing focus. A channel of the player's holds it still until the channel ends (see "Abilities and effects"). How a walk meets a door is under "Doors".

Paths come from `findPath` in `packages/core/src/pathfinding.ts`, an A* search over walk rasters. The rasters share one grid of 0.25 m cells anchored at the space's origin, so the cells of neighbouring sectors line up and a path crosses a border on shared neighbours. A sector's raster holds the cells whose centres lie in its rect, each with a bit for a clear centre and one for each of its four forward edges, from the static world alone. `ClientWorld` computes it when that sector's frame arrives and again when a touching sector's does, since an edge with no held neighbour blocks. It keeps a sector's raster until the one for its current inputs replaces it, and drops a result asked for before a later one. A sector with no raster yet blocks every search, so a walk asked for just after `enterSpace` waits for it. The rasters are computed in a module worker (`walkRasterizer.ts`), inline where there is no worker or it fails, through the `workerJobs` helper (`packages/web/src/workerJobs.ts`) the ground field uses too. A search looks within the start and the goal plus 10 m, under an expansion cap, and plans round a disk about every NPC, nightmare, and other dreamer the client draws within 16 m. A goal inside such disks is moved to the nearest point outside them all, on the edge of one: contact plus `bodyMargin`. Of points as near, the one nearest the walker wins, so a goal at a body's very centre, where a press on that body aims the walk, moves straight out toward the walker. That point lies within touch (contact plus `COMBAT.reachSlack`), so a tap beside a fallen dreamer or a nightmare, or a press on one, ends in reach of them, which a Healer raising the fallen needs. The path ends at the goal wherever the goal's cell is clear and a leg reaches it, even where a body's disk covers the goal's cell. That leg starts at the goal's cell where the search reaches it. Otherwise it starts at whichever lies nearest the goal with a leg to it: the start, the expanded cell nearest the goal, or an expanded cell within 1 m of it (`GOAL_APPROACH`). Where the goal cannot be reached, the path ends at the reachable cell nearest it.

Every part of the pathfinder judges a step by one rule, `legWalkable`: a leg is walkable when every sample along it is clear at the full `playerRadius` and it never deepens its overlap with the bodies, taken together (`bodiesBlockLine`, the follower's `bodiesBlock` at any spacing of its steps). With one body that means never nearing its centre within contact. Taken together, a walker pressed between two it overlaps on opposite sides can still step out between them, where judged one by one every way out nears one of them. Bodies are judged with `bodyMargin` added where a leg starts clear of them all by that margin, and without it otherwise. The rule uses the full radius, not `isLegalMove`'s path tolerance, because the follower tests each step at the full radius and would stop dead on a corner a leg grazes within the tolerance. The search's edges, the string-pulling of cells into legs, the goal's end point, and the retry without an edge a leg found unwalkable all apply it, so no part hands another a step it refuses, and a walk can always leave the bodies it overlaps.

Facing is independent of travel. On a fine pointer the player faces the point under the cursor at body height, 0.8 m above their feet (`WorldScene.aimPointAt`), so pointing at a body drawn above its feet aims at it, or their target where it is drawn while the gaze is locked on it. A finger has no cursor, so on a coarse pointer they face the way they walk, and their target only while the gaze is locked on it. The gait is a jog. Run, a toggle on the HUD and on Shift that holds across spaces for the session, reports `run`, and a winded player walks whatever it says. A braced one jogs (see "Abilities and effects").

The server accepts a move when its length fits the player's allowance and `isLegalMove` passes (colliders, blockers, ledges, the step-height limit, NPCs); otherwise it answers `correction {x, z}` with the last accepted position. The allowance accrues on the server's clock at a little over running speed and holds two seconds' worth. Length is checked first: a move longer than the allowance is refused and spends nothing, and a move the allowance covers spends its length whether or not its path is legal. The predictor never reports a position its own `isLegalMove` refuses, so an honest client that stays standing is corrected only when its reports were held back for longer than the allowance holds and then arrive together. Players are not checked against each other or against monsters on the server: each client stops its own player at the others where it draws them.

Accepted moves and monster steps go out batched in a `moves` frame per connection every 100 ms, which the client interpolates. The decoder caps a frame at `maxMovesPerFrame` moves, so when more moved in view the server splits the batch across frames. A monster is in it only when it moved or turned.

**Doors.** `useDoor {sector, doorId}` moves the player to the counterpart door's space, and is answered with `doorRefused` when the player's space has no such live door, the player stands outside its trigger (widened by `doorUseSlack`), the player lies fallen, or the door is `nightOnly` and the sun is up. Such a door is open exactly while `sunIsUp` in `packages/core/src/worldClock.ts` says the sun is down, from 22:00 to 06:00 (`doorOpenAt` in `world.ts`), the same hours the sky and the HUD's clock show, so the three cannot disagree. A client refused at a door its own clock has shut says in chat that the door opens only at night; any other refusal says nothing. The client sends `useDoor` when a walk heading through the door steps into the trigger and then reports nothing until `enterSpace` or `doorRefused` arrives. A path takes the door it ends at, which ends it, since its destination lies in the space left; a path that only passes a door walks on through its trigger. A steer takes a door on entering its trigger, and from within it only on stepping toward the doorway, so a player a path left standing in a trigger can steer back out. A refused door is not asked for again until a step has left its trigger. A press still held through a door steers on toward the ground under the pointer once the player stands in the space it leads to. The two doors of every committed pair face opposite ways, so that steer carries the player away from the door they arrive by.

**Acting.** Walking into an entity asks nothing of the server. An NPC greets a player who comes within speaking distance (`speakingDistance`) with the first step of its script, unasked: once per approach, and not again within `npcGreetingPauseSeconds`. `interact {carrierId}` asks it to go on. It says the steps after the greeting to that player, the first at once and the rest a cooldown apart. On a fixture `interact` only uses it. Either way it counts as a visit to the carrier (see "Quests"), and it is dropped from a fallen player and from beyond speaking distance. `carrierId` names a carrier, an NPC by its entity id or a fixture by its carrier id, here and in every request to a service (see "Fixtures and services").

`target {targetId?}` chooses the entity a player acts on, the player themselves included, or none. One out of the player's view is dropped silently. Choosing does nothing to the entity, and a fallen player may still choose. The server sends that player `vitals {entityId, health}`, a share of the maximum to two decimals, for their target, never for themselves, since their `energy` already says it: at once for one newly targeted, then on a change, at most every 0.25 s. An NPC is always whole. A target that leaves the player's view is let go on both sides. Targets are per space and not saved.

`engage {on}` switches slot 1 on or off. Off is always taken. On is taken only from a standing player whose target slot 1 can act on at that moment (`engageAction` in `packages/core/src/combat.ts`): a standing nightmare in view, with the main hand empty or holding a weapon and no quest held that forbids striking (`withoutStriking`, as the Healers' trial does), or a dreamer in view, the player included, for a Healer with the Moonstone in the off hand. Each hand does its own job, so a Healer holding a weapon and the Moonstone attacks a nightmare and mends a dreamer, by the target. A refusal is dropped silently. While it is on at a nightmare, the server swings at the target alone, at the swing rhythm, and only while the target stands in reach inside the swing arc of the facing last reported (`swingMeets`). Nothing else in reach is struck. A swing not made, with the target out of reach, behind the player, or more balance due than they have, costs nothing. While it is on at a standing dreamer, the server mends them once per touch interval while they stand within `COMBAT.mend.reach` (3 m, centre to centre, `mendReaches`), for `COMBAT.mend.spiritCost` each. Short of spirit, or with the dreamer at full health, it waits and stays on. A Healer who targets themselves mends themselves this way. Slot 1 stays on when the target changes to another it can act on. It goes off when the target is driven off, leaves view, or gives way to one it cannot act on, when a `target` is refused, when the Moonstone leaves the off hand of a player whose target is a dreamer or the main hand takes what does not swing or the player takes on a quest that forbids striking while the target is a nightmare, and when the player falls. Like the target, it is per space and not saved.

On a fallen dreamer, Mend raises instead, by touch: within `reaches`, contact plus `COMBAT.reachSlack`. Every Healer engaged on them within touch raises them together. Beginning a raise needs a rank of `drawing-back` and the spirit a whole raise costs on hand, so a lone Healer can finish it. Joining one under way needs the rank, a mend's worth of spirit, and room among the `maxRaiseHealers` Healers one raise holds at most. A dreamer the toggle mends who falls keeps it on, since Mend raises them. Every mend, on another player or on oneself, goes out as `mend {targetId}` to each player who sees the mended player. It carries no amount, because a player learns another's health only as its `condition`, or as `vitals` while they target them. Nothing a player sends harms another player.

**Abilities and effects.** `useAbility {abilityId}` uses an ability on the belt. Like `engage`, it names no target: the server judges it against the target it holds, by `abilityAllowed` in `packages/core/src/abilities.ts`, the rule the client reads too. It is taken only from a standing player who channels nothing, holds the ability's teaching, holds what its kit needs in hand, has its cost in its pool, is past its own cooldown and the global one (`GLOBAL_COOLDOWN_SECONDS`), and has a recipient for it. A refusal is dropped silently.

`abilityRecipient` finds the recipient, at a range measured beyond contact. An `enemy` ability lands on the target while it is a standing nightmare in range, a `self` ability on the user, and an `area` ability on the standing bodies of its kind round them (`areaRecipients`). An `ally` ability lands on the target while it is a dreamer, the user included, and on the user while the target is no dreamer: a nightmare, an NPC, or nothing. At a fallen dreamer, whom only slot 1 raises, or one out of range, it lands on nobody and is refused, so a Healer never heals themselves by accident. Resolving the recipient changes neither the target nor the gaze lock.

What a hand allows is `handAllows` in `packages/core/src/combat.ts`, which `engageAction` reads too, so slot 1 and the belt never disagree about it: `mainHand` is the main hand empty or holding a weapon, which every Fighter ability needs, `moonstone` the Moonstone in the off hand, which every Healer ability needs, and `focus` a focus in the off hand (the wand or the skull staff), which every Mystic ability needs. Slot 1 is no ability: its swings and mends keep the swing rhythm and the touch interval, and neither start nor wait on a cooldown. A use never switches slot 1 on or off.

A use spends its cost and starts its own cooldown, by its rank, and the global one. Everyone who sees the user is sent `ability {entityId, abilityId, targetId?, evaded?}`, and their client plays the ability's clip on the user. `targetId` names the recipient when it is not the user, and `evaded` is set on harm its nightmare evades (see Dream-bolt). The user alone is sent `cooldowns {globalSeconds, cooldowns}`, the seconds left on each cooldown running, after each use and after every `enterSpace`. The client starts the global cooldown itself on a press and uses no ability again until that answer arrives, or for 1 s, since a refusal gets none. `GameplayPredictor` holds its last position behind the 100 ms heartbeat, so a request the server judges by where the player stands calls `flush()` first, which sends the held-back report at once. `useAbility` is such a request.

Deep mend is the one channelled ability (`channelSeconds` in `ABILITIES`). Every other takes effect the moment it is used, except that Dream-bolt's harm lands as the bolt arrives (see Dream-bolt). A channelled use spends its cost and starts its cooldowns at its start, and lands only when its seconds, shorter at each rank, have run. Its `ability` frames carry a `phase`: `start` with the channel's length in `seconds`, then `done` as it lands or `broken`, each to everyone who sees the caster. An instant use carries no `phase`. The decoder requires `seconds` with `start` and `channel` and refuses it with any other phase or none (`ABILITY_PHASES`). A dreamer who chooses the caster as their target while a channel is under way is sent it as `channel` with the seconds left, as `effects` are; the caster knows their own. No caster joins a space mid-channel, since a door breaks it, so `attach` replays none.

A channel breaks, and lands nothing, when a `move` reports a position other than the last accepted one, accepted or not; a `move` that only turns the facing leaves it be. It also breaks on a door transfer or a wake, when the caster falls, and when the recipient falls, leaves the space, or is out of the caster's view as it would land. While it runs, slot 1 stays on but holds its swings, mends, and raises, and every other ability is refused (`abilityAllowed` reads `channelling`). A space step lands its channels before slot 1 acts, so slot 1 acts again in the step a channel ends: a Healer who channels on one dreamer and then targets a fallen one with slot 1 on takes hold of the raise only then. The client's `useAbility` for a channel calls `flush()`, and the walk under way, a held press's steer included, then waits with the player standing (`heldStill` in `predictor.ts`), so no later report breaks the channel. The walk is kept: a held pointer still moves where the steer heads, and letting it go turns the steer into the walk to where it came up. As the channel lands or breaks, or after `ABILITY_ANSWER_TIMEOUT_MS` (1 s) with no answer, since a refused use gets none, the walk goes on: a press still held steers on toward the ground under the pointer, a released one walks to where it came up, and a path, a pick-up's arrival included, walks on from where the player stands. Only a new press ends the waiting walk, and the walk it makes breaks the channel.

`effects {entityId, effects}` lists the effects an entity holds (`EFFECTS` in `packages/core/src/effects.ts`): each timed one with its `seconds` left, and an elite's trait, which lasts as long as its holder, without them. It goes to the holder and to every player whose target the holder is, whenever an effect starts, starts over, or ends. Like `vitals`, it also goes at once to a player for the target they choose, empty for a target holding none. No one else is sent effects, since nothing else draws them. An effect applied again starts its seconds over rather than stacking. Effects and cooldowns are not saved, but they carry through a door and a wake. A door, a wake, and a disconnect each take the player out through `SpaceActor.handOver`. It hands back their checkpoint, to be attached elsewhere or saved, and for each effect its seconds left, its rank, what is left of a shield, and when a heal over time next gives health, with the fraction it owes. So a door makes no worn ward whole and costs a Renewal nothing. `transferPlayer` passes the effects to the destination's `attach`, or to the restore after a failed transfer. `handOver` settles the effects first and reads the checkpoint after, so a tick of a heal over time that fell due as they left is in that checkpoint.

Harm reaches a dreamer through `SpaceActor.harmDreamer` alone, and a nightmare through `harmNightmare`. Each lands `harmTaken`: the amount by the `damageDealt` share of each effect its source holds and the `damageTaken` share of each effect its taker holds, rounded once, half up (`wholeAmount`), and never less than 1, since pools are whole numbers. A shield the taker holds (an effect with `absorb`, Ward's on a dreamer or a Shielded elite's) takes what it can of that share before it is rounded, and the rest lands, whole and never less than 1 (`shieldedHarm`). A hit the shield takes all of lands nothing, its balance included, and that is the only way a hit lands 0. The shield wears down by what it took and ends once spent. `harmDreamer` holds the fall. `harmNightmare` raises the source's threat by what landed and drives the nightmare off at none; slot 1's swings, Cleave's, and Dream-bolt all go through it. A nightmare driven off splits its bounty, and gives the practice and the count toward a drive-off objective in full, among the standing dreamers within `COMBAT.shareRadius` of it and the standing dreamer whose harm drove it off, from however far.

Health reaches a standing dreamer from a Healer through `SpaceActor.heal` alone: slot 1's mend, Deep mend, Renewal's seconds, and Radiance. It gives as much of the amount, made whole, as they miss, and raises threat and earns practice by what it gave (see "Threat"), unless the Healer has left the space. Only the raise stands a fallen dreamer up: a heal gives a fallen dreamer nothing, and a fall ends every heal over time its holder holds.

The Fighter's kit:

- Cleave swings at every standing nightmare within touch that the swing arc meets, each a `blow` for a share of the swing's damage.
- Challenge puts `challenged` on every nightmare within 5 m of the Fighter beyond contact, which turns it on them (see "Threat").
- Brace puts `braced` on the Fighter: half the harm, and no faster than a jog. That is `gaitAllowed` in `combat.ts`: the predictor jogs whatever Run says, and the server reads each `move`'s gait through it for the balance drain and the `moves` frame. The allowance is not shortened, so a client a frame late to apply Brace is never corrected.
- Lunge leaps to the targeted nightmare, at most 3 m beyond contact. The leap starts when the player's own `ability` frame arrives, toward the body it names where the client draws it then, so a use the server refuses moves no one. The client moves the player through `resolveMove` as a walk of its own (`leap` in `predictor.ts`), which replaces any walk under way and is reported as moves. A leap heads for a body, so it never takes a door. The ordinary allowance carries it, holding 7.5 m against the leap's 3, and Lunge's cooldown refills what one leap spends.
- Rally gives Balance back to every standing dreamer within a said line's reach, 12 m centre to centre.

The Healer's kit, each ability spending Spirit:

- Deep mend channels on its `ally` recipient for 1.5 s at rank 1, then heals them.
- Renewal puts `renewing` on its `ally` recipient, a heal over time: health once a second for 10 s, the fraction of a whole carried from one second to the next.
- Radiance heals every standing dreamer within 5 m of the Healer, centre to centre, the Healer included.
- Calm cuts the Healer's threat on every nightmare's table to half at once, then puts `calmed` on the Healer, which halves the threat they raise while it holds.

The Mystic's kit, each ability spending Spirit:

- Ward puts `warded` on its `ally` recipient: a shield that takes harm in their place until spent or for 12 s, holding more at each rank.
- Weaken puts `weakened` on the targeted nightmare, whose harm is dealt at its `damageDealt` share while it holds.
- Bind puts `bound` on the targeted nightmare, which roots it: it neither chases nor walks home, turns toward whom it would chase, and still strikes whoever stands in its reach.
- Dream-bolt harms the targeted nightmare, up to 8 m beyond contact, through `harmNightmare`. It rolls no hit, so it never misses, and sends no `blow`. The cost, the cooldowns, and the `ability` frame come at the use. The harm, and the threat with it, lands when the bolt arrives, after `boltFlightSeconds` in `packages/core/src/abilities.ts`: the clip's release plus the distance centre to centre at the use over the bolt's speed. A bolt whose nightmare was driven off or is gone by then, or whose caster has left the space, lands nothing. One whose caster fell meanwhile still lands. A nightmare evades a bolt from a Mystic standing outside its home ground, where it would not chase them. The bolt then lands nothing, its threat included, though the cost and the cooldowns are spent. Evasion is judged where the Mystic stands at the use, not as the bolt lands, so the `ability` frame can carry `evaded` and the client shows the miss word where the bolt arrives. `SpaceActor.evades` judges it, and any later harm from afar should go through it too.
- Veil puts `veiled` on every standing dreamer within 5 m of the Mystic, centre to centre, the Mystic included. Its `unseen` takes them off every nightmare's table and out of whom it chases and strikes while it holds.
- An effect an ability puts on a nightmare also puts its caster on that nightmare's table at no threat, so a Weaken or a Bind turns it on the Mystic unless it holds more against another.

**Sense the fallen** is a passive of the Mystic's. A player holding its rank is sent `sensed {bearings}`, each bearing a fallen dreamer's `name` and `x`/`z` in the space. A frame is whole: every other fallen dreamer in the space, nearest first, at most `maxSensedBearings`, and empty when none lies fallen. Sector interest plays no part. It goes whenever a dreamer in the space falls, is raised, or arrives or leaves fallen, since a fallen dreamer does not move. It also goes as a snapshot when the Mystic becomes able to sense, on joining a space (a door included) and on learning the rank, even with nobody fallen, so a dreamer already lying fallen is pinned at once.

**Threat.** Each nightmare holds threat against the dreamers on its table. Harm a dreamer lands on it adds that much against them. A heal of a dreamer it holds threat against, from slot 1 or an ability, adds half the health given (`COMBAT.threatPerHealthMended`) against the Healer, shared among the nightmares not driven off that hold threat against the dreamer healed. Each amount is scaled by the doer's effects (`threatRaised`), so a calmed Healer raises half. Calm also halves at once what every nightmare holds against them. A nightmare drops from its table a dreamer who falls, leaves the space, steps outside its home ground, or is unseen under a Veil, though a Challenge it holds from them runs on until it ends. It chases the foremost of the standing dreamers inside its home ground who are past a raise's grace, not unseen, and within its aggro radius or on its table: one whose Challenge it holds, else the one it holds the most threat against, else the nearest. It strikes the dreamer it chases once in reach. Otherwise it strikes the foremost standing dreamer in its reach who is not unseen, who may stand just outside the ground, so a Challenge holds at the strike too. A bound nightmare stays where it stands, turning toward whom it would chase, and strikes all the same.

**Elites.** A monster spawn's `eliteChance` (see "Sector format") is the chance that a monster it spawns is an elite, rolled from the space's own `eliteRandom`, so an elite never shifts the rolls of combat and placement. An elite rolls one trait of `ELITE_TRAITS` in `packages/core/src/monsterKinds.ts`, from the same `eliteRandom`, and spawns with `ELITE_HEALTH_SHARE` (2.5) times its kind's health (`nightmareHealth`), which it rests back up to. Each trait is an effect of `EFFECTS` the elite holds as long as it stands, with no seconds on the wire, so every harm meets it in `harmNightmare`, whether slot 1's swing, Cleave's, or a Dream-bolt. Stony (`stony`) takes 0.6 of the harm. Shielded (`shielded`) holds a shield of 15 that takes harm first, as a ward does, and grows back whole 8 s (`renewSeconds`) after the step that finds it spent. A hit the shield takes all of still puts the dreamer on the elite's table. Its `entity` frame carries `rank: 'elite'` and `trait`, the trait's id, both left out for an ordinary nightmare. `rank` is the wire vocabulary `NIGHTMARE_RANKS`: `elite` and `boss`. An elite drops by `DROP_ODDS.elite` (see "Drops").

**Bosses.** A kind in `BOSS_MOVES` (`packages/core/src/monsterKinds.ts`), the Necromancer alone, spawns as a boss, never as an elite: `rank: 'boss'` with no trait, its kind's own health, and drops by `DROP_ODDS.boss`. Its spawn, the burial chamber's, keeps one alive (`maxAlive` 1) and refills only after its kind's long respawn (`respawnSeconds`, 10 minutes), though every spawn first fills within a minute of its space starting. It strikes in reach as any nightmare does, and once it has a dreamer to chase it has two moves of its own (`runBoss` in `SpaceActor`):

- **The wail.** 4 s into a fight (`firstWailSeconds`), and 10 s after each wail struck (`wailEverySeconds`), while a dreamer stands in the circle it would strike, it telegraphs a wail (`TELEGRAPHS.wail`), a circle of 4 m round where it stands. 2.5 s later the wail strikes every dreamer standing in it, centre within the radius, for 45 health and 15 balance, through `harmDreamer`. It spares those nightmares leave alone: the fallen, those in a raise's grace, and the unseen. It sends no `blow`. A lethal wail fells a dreamer exactly as a strike does. The boss holds still and strikes no one until the wail strikes, and a boss driven off ends its wail unstruck. Everyone who sees its sector is sent `telegraph {id, casterId, kind, x, z, radius, seconds}` as it begins, and a dreamer who comes into view of one under way, by joining or by changing sector, is sent it with the seconds left, as a raise's snapshot is. Its end sends nothing: the client ends a circle as it strikes, or as its caster is driven off or leaves view. `kind` is the wire vocabulary `TELEGRAPH_KINDS`, whose one member is `wail`, and `TELEGRAPHS` gives each kind's length to both sides, so a client told of one part-way draws it as full as it is.
- **Minions.** As a fight begins and every 15 s while it fights, never twice within 15 s (`callEverySeconds`), it calls up two Skeletons (`perCall`), never more than four of its own standing (`maxAlive`), each at a free point of its home ground between 1 and 3 m from it. A call falling due during a wail waits for the wail to strike. A minion takes the boss's home ground as its own, counts against no spawn timer, and waits a strike's time before its first. It is sent on its arriving `entity` frame alone with `summonedBy`, the boss's id, so its viewers play the boss's call and the minion's arrival. Driven off by a dreamer, a minion falls, pays, and drops as any nightmare does, and touches no spawn. The minions last as long as the boss's fight. A boss with no one to chase walks home at once, but gives its fight up only once it has had no one to chase for 5 s (`giveUpSeconds`), so a dreamer who steps out of its ground and back within that finds its minions, its wail, and its next call as they were. Given up, it holds back its first wail as at the start of a fight. As it gives up, and as it is driven off, every minion of its own still standing crumbles: it is gone at once, never `fallen`, paying no one and dropping nothing, and everyone who sees it is sent `leave {entityId, leftGame: false, crumbled: true}`.

**Gear.** A dreamer carries coins and items (`Inventory` in `packages/core/src/domain/inventory.ts`). Each item is an instance of its own: a base from `BASE_ITEMS` (`packages/core/src/items.ts`, each with its kind of slot, tier, and base stats), the tier it rolled at, a rarity from `RARITIES`, and the stat lines it rolled from `AFFIXES`. Tier 1 has a base of every kind. Tier 3 adds stronger weapons, shields, and a focus (`bone-dagger`, `sabre`, `war-axe`, `iron-buckler`, `skull-shield`, `skull-staff`) and no armour or jewellery, which drop there as tier-1 bases rolled at tier 3. `rollItem` in `packages/core/src/affixes.ts` rolls none for a plain item, one or two for a magic one, and three or four for a rare one, no line twice, each within its range at the tier it rolls at, whatever its base's tier. Every line has a range at tiers 1 and 3, higher at 3, and none at tier 2, which no nightmare drops. Each kind of slot rolls its own lines: armour mostly Health, Guard, and Balance, a weapon and gloves the swing's, and the off hand and jewellery Spirit, recovery, mends, and wards. Every kind of slot offers at least a rare's four. The Moonstone is never rolled. A dreamer has the ten slots of `EQUIP_SLOTS`: the main hand, the off hand, head, chest, hands, legs, feet, neck, and two rings. A base's kind of slot is one of them or `ring`, which fits `ring1` and `ring2` alike, and `slotsFor` is the one rule of where a base is worn, which the server, the client's `inventoryOf`, and the item repository all read. An item is worn in a slot its base fits, one item to a slot, or carried at a place in the bag, 0 to 23, one item to a place, which the player arranges. Its id is a UUIDv7 (`newItemId`). The server saves them with each checkpoint, as the `items` table, each row with exactly one of `equipped_slot` and `bag_place`, and `characters.coins`. The item repository refuses a place outside the bag or held twice.

`inventory {coins, equipped, bag, trade?, dropped?, pickedUp?, rewarded?}` carries all of it to the player alone, whole in every frame: `equipped` as `{slot, item}` in slot order, a slot at most once, `bag` as `{place, item}` in place order, a place at most once, and each item as `{id, baseId, tier, rarity, affixes: [{affixId, value}]}`. `inventoryMessage` and `inventoryOf` turn an `Inventory` into the frame and back, and the client keeps only the items whose base, tier, and affixes it knows, each worn in a slot its base fits. The rules of where an item goes are core's (`packages/core/src/domain/inventory.ts`), which the server applies and the client reads before it asks.

`equip {itemId, slot?}` wears an item in `slot`, one its base fits, or else in the slot `equipSlot` chooses, the first of its slots that is empty, else the first: a second ring goes on `ring2`, and a third replaces the one on `ring1`. From the bag, whatever the slot wore takes the item's place there, so wearing needs no room. A ring worn in one ring slot moves to the other, changing slots with what that one wears (`equipItem`). `unequip {slot}` puts what the slot wears at the bag's first free place (`unequipItem`). `moveItem {itemId, place}` moves an item to a place in the bag (`moveItem`): from another place, onto an occupied one the two change places; a worn item is put away there, or changes places with the item there when that one fits its slot, which needs no room, or else goes to the first free place. Every new item in the bag, a pick-up, a purchase, and a trial's reward with its slot taken, goes to the first free place (`withItem`). The bag holds at most `maxBagItems` (24), and `bagHasRoom` is the one rule every grant of an item reads, `unequip` and a worn item's `moveItem` included: a full bag refuses it, so the bag never passes the cap and its frame always decodes. The client greys out the Unequip on a worn item's card by the same rule.

Everyone who sees a dreamer learns only what their hands hold: `held {mainHand?, offHand?}`, base ids, on their `entity` frame, and `held {entityId, held}` whenever either hand changes, which the client draws as hand models (see "Web asset pack").

`statsOf` in `packages/core/src/combat.ts` folds a dreamer's ranks and what they wear into one `Stats`, which the rules read: the swing, a nightmare's chance to land a strike, the pool maxima, both recoveries, a mend's depth, and a ward's shield. The maxima follow every change to what a dreamer wears. What slot 1 and the belt can do follows the two hands (see "Acting" and `handAllows`).

**Trading.** `buy {carrierId, baseId}` and `sell {carrierId, itemId}` are requests to the `shopkeeper`, under the gate of every service (see "Fixtures and services"). A purchase is a plain tier-1 item of a ware's base, and a sale names an item in the bag by its id. The wares are every tier-1 base but the Moonstone, and the shopkeeper buys an item of any tier. The wares, their prices, and what an item sells for by its rarity and tier are `SHOP` in `packages/core/src/shop.ts`, and its `canBuy`, `salePrice`, and `sellableItem` are the rules both sides read. A player buys only what their purse covers into a bag with room, and sells anything in the bag, so an item worn is taken off before it is sold. The purse and the Moonstone are never traded. A refused trade is dropped silently. A trade is answered with one `inventory` frame carrying `trade {kind, baseId, rarity, coins}`, so the purse and the bag change together. The client announces the trade from it instead of the coins gained. A trade is saved by the next checkpoint, like every other inventory change. Mercus's panel draws each ware, and each item in the bag it buys, as the item's icon edged in its rarity's colour beside its name.

**Drops.** When a nightmare is driven off, each dreamer who shares it (the standing dreamers within `COMBAT.shareRadius` and the one whose harm drove it off) rolls for drops of their own, apart from the others, by `rollDrops` in `packages/core/src/loot.ts`: the nightmare's `tier` (`MonsterKind.tier`, the bestiary's ring) and the rank of foe (`DROP_ODDS`) give how many rolls, each roll's chance, each rarity's weight, and the bases it may be (`droppableBases`): every base of that tier or a lower one, each as likely, never the Moonstone. An ordinary nightmare (`nightmare`) gives one roll at 0.3, an `elite` two at 0.5 that lean to magic and rare, and a `boss` four at 0.9, mostly magic and rare. Every drop is rolled at the nightmare's tier, whatever its base's. The loot is smart: a drop is of a base the dreamer's role can use (`usableBy`), but for `DROP_ODDS`' open share (one in five), which may be any of those bases, so something to sell or give still turns up. Every main hand, armour piece, and piece of jewellery serves every role. The off hand of a role whose trial gives an off-hand item (`TRIAL_REWARDS`) is held by that kind of item, as `handAllows` judges it, and any other role's by an off hand no kit needs: a Fighter rolls no focus, a Healer neither a focus nor a shield, and a Mystic no shield. A dreamer with no role rolls from every base. The rolls come from the space's own `lootRandom`, so a drop never shifts the rolls of combat and placement.

Each sharer who holds a quest still collecting the nightmare's kind (`collectsTrophy` in `packages/core/src/quests.ts`) also finds that kind's trophy, one drop more of their own (`TROPHIES`: a catalog name for each kind and nothing else, no base, tier, or rarity).

A drop is a server-held record holding the whole rolled item or the trophy, for its owner alone: they are sent `loot {lootId, x, z, contents, from}`, and no one else hears of it. It lies scattered from where the nightmare stood, clear of the bodies fighting over it, at the point `throwLanding` gives (`scatterLandings`). Every drop one nightmare leaves, all its sharers' together, is fanned evenly round it from a heading rolled from `lootRandom`, each nudged on by up to half its share of the turn, so no two lie in one heap, and each `SCATTER_DISTANCE` (1 to 2 m, rolled) away. `from {x, z}` says where the nightmare stood, so the owner's client pops the drop out from there, and comes only on that first frame. `contents` is a tagged union on `kind`, decoded with `requireStringEnum` against `LOOT_KINDS`: `{kind: 'item', baseId, rarity}`, of which the owner sees the base and the rarity, not the stat lines, until they pick it up, or `{kind: 'trophy', monsterKind}`. `pickUp {lootId}` takes the owner's own drop, from a standing dreamer within `PICKUP_REACH` (1 m, centre to the drop's point): an item into their bag, with room in it (`bagHasRoom`), and a trophy whatever the bag holds, since it never enters the bag. Anything else is dropped silently and the drop stays where it lies. A pick-up is answered with `lootGone {lootId}` and then one `inventory` frame carrying `pickedUp`, the drop's contents, which the client announces in chat. An item comes into the bag under a new id. A trophy counts toward each `collect` objective of its kind the player holds, and a quest given up drops its count.

A drop has no timer: it lies until its owner leaves the world, and waits for them through doors and wakes. Each join of its space (a door back, a wake into it, the restore after a failed transfer) sends its owner a `loot` for each of theirs lying there, without `from`, after the entities. At most `MAX_PERSONAL_DROPS` (50) lie for one dreamer across the world, and a new one past that takes their oldest away wherever it lies, with `lootGone` if they stand in its space. One `PersonalDrops` ledger (`packages/server/src/world/personalDrops.ts`), which the router hands every `SpaceActor`, counts them in the order they fell and tells a space which to let go. `WorldRouter.unregister`, the one place an account leaves the router, after its checkpoint is written, takes away every drop lying for the character that joined, without a frame. Drops are never saved, so a server restart clears them.

`dropItem {itemId, heading}` throws an item the dreamer carries or wears to the ground, whole, its stat lines with it, for anyone, as Diablo drops one. `heading` is a heading in degrees, decoded as any finite number as `move`'s `facing` is: toward where a drag let the item go over the world, else toward the cursor on a fine pointer (`GameplaySession.dropItem`, the point the player faces), else on a finger the way the player faces. The client turns the player to it and reports that facing (`flush()`) before the request, so everyone sees them face the throw. The server lays the item at `throwLanding` (`packages/core/src/loot.ts`), `THROW_DISTANCE` (1 m, within the thrower's reach) from the dreamer's centre along the heading. Where that point is blocked, the item lands at the farthest point short of it that the throw reaches with room for a drop all the way out (0.1 m clear of every wall, collider, and ledge, inside the space). That point must also be one a dreamer standing where the thrower stood comes within `PICKUP_REACH` of by a legal step along the throw. Where no point qualifies, the item lands at the thrower's feet. So a throw at a wall lands on the thrower's side of it, and one through a gap no body passes lands within reach. `droppableItem` is the rule both sides read: anything carried or worn but the Moonstone, and a fallen dreamer drops nothing. A worn item leaves its slot in the same step, so it needs no room in the bag. Anything else is dropped silently.

The item leaves the bag or its slot at once. Everyone who sees where it lands is sent its `loot` first, then the dropper one `inventory` frame carrying `dropped`, its contents, which the client announces in chat, and then, for an item that left a hand, everyone who sees the dropper the `held` that empties it, so every client knows of the throw before it learns the hand is empty.

The dropper's checkpoint is handed at once to the router's `DropSaves` (`packages/server/src/world/checkpointWriter.ts`), through `SpaceActor`'s `requestDropSave` option, without the throw waiting for it, so the item leaves their rows ahead of the next periodic checkpoint. That matters because a picker's disconnect writes the item straight away under a new id, and a crash before the dropper's rows lost it would leave it in both characters' rows. That window stays open from the throw until the dropper's save lands, and a picker's save committing inside it followed by a crash still leaves the item in both. A failed save, logged with `origin: 'drop'`, keeps it open until the dropper's next successful save, periodic or on leaving. The owner accepted that window over making the throw wait for the database. `DropSaves` keeps at most one save in flight per character, keyed by character id so every space a dreamer passes through shares it. A save asked for meanwhile waits in one pending slot, which a later throw replaces with its newer snapshot, and is written once the one in flight settles, whether it succeeded or failed. So a loop of throws and pick-ups queues at most two writes per dreamer rather than one per frame. A disconnect drops the pending save, since its own snapshot is newer, and waits for the one in flight to settle before it writes its own and lets the account go, so a relogin never reads rows a drop save is about to replace. Each checkpoint of a player, and the write a join makes, carries a `lastSeen` strictly later than the one before it, a millisecond on where the clock has not moved or has stepped back (`nextLastSeen` in `packages/core/src/domain/character.ts`), so the `last_seen` guard orders two snapshots taken in one millisecond, and a join after the host clock stepped back, rather than skipping the later. Every checkpoint write names its writer in `origin`: `periodic`, `disconnect`, or `drop`.

A dropped item lies for `SHARED_LOOT_SECONDS` (15 minutes) on the space's clock as a record with no owner, shown under sector interest like an entity. `loot` with `shared: true` goes to every dreamer who sees its sector as it falls. That frame carries `from {x, z, entityId, hand?}`: where the thrower stood, who they are, and the hand the item left when it left one (`HANDS`: `mainHand` or `offHand`). The `loot` goes again at a join and as its sector comes into a dreamer's view. `lootGone` goes as the sector leaves their view, as the item is picked up, and as it expires. At most `MAX_SHARED_DROPS` (100) dropped items lie in one space, and a new one past that takes the space's oldest away, with `lootGone` to everyone who sees it, so a pile can never flood the frames a join sends. Drops from nightmares are not counted. Any standing dreamer within `PICKUP_REACH` with room in the bag picks it up by the same `pickUp`, and the first to ask takes it. It stays on the ground when its dropper leaves the space, and like every drop it is not saved, so a server restart loses it.

The client draws each drop as Diablo does (`packages/web/src/scene/lootMarkers.ts`): the item's own ground model lying where it fell (see "Web asset pack"), turned by a yaw hashed from its `lootId`, so neighbouring drops do not line up and one drop lies the same way each time it is drawn, and its name centred under it on screen in its rarity's colour, through the world. The name's top edge stands `LOOT_LABEL_GAP` (0.12 m) under the lowest point of the model as drawn, or of the drop's point while it has no model, so it covers none of the model and stands below the feet of a body standing on the drop. A rare drop raises a soft gold beam, additive and lit by no light, which brightens and dims over 2.4 s and holds steady under reduced motion. A magic or a rare drop glows faintly on the ground under its model in its colour, and a plain one has neither. A trophy has no model, so its name alone is drawn, as plain. A drop whose model is not loaded yet, or whose base has none, draws its name alone, which still takes a press, and lays its model once `WorldScene.prewarm` has loaded the models.

A `loot` frame carrying `from` is the drop falling, and every client it reaches throws it there (`WorldScene.showLoot`). The dreamer `from.entityId` names plays the throw (`Throw`, `ONE_SHOT_CLIPS.throw`), started part-way in so the item leaves their hand within `THROW_WINDUP_SECONDS` (0.25 s) or at the clip's release (`STRIKE_AT`), whichever comes first. The item flies from that hand, or from 0.4 m over `from` where there is none, in a parabola 0.35 m over its line, turning over once in 0.45 s, and hops 6 cm as it lands (`THROW_ARC`). A nightmare's drop bursts out as the blow that drove it off lands, as Diablo's loot does: from 0.55 m over `from`, about a Ghost's chest, in a parabola 1 m over its line, turning over twice in 0.65 s, and it hops 10 cm (`BURST_ARC`). The server sends that blow, the nightmare's fall, and its drops in one batch, so the drop's flight waits for the last hit still on its way to the fallen nightmare drawn nearest `from` (`CombatEffects.hitsLandIn`), and every drop it leaves bursts together, in the frame the nightmare's fall starts (see "Combat effects land with the clip"). With none on its way, as after a Dream-bolt, whose harm the server lands as the bolt arrives, it bursts at once. A drop shows its name, its glow, and its beam only as it lands, and until then its name takes no press. A drop lying still presses the grass under its model flat, easing out past its edge, and the grass springs back once the drop goes; a drop in the air presses none. An item `from.hand` names, which that hand draws, is handed to the throw. Its hand model stays on the hand through the wind-up, whatever a `held` arriving meanwhile says of that hand. A change that `held` makes to the other hand shows at once. In the frame of the release, the hand model leaves the hand as the ground model takes its place, drawn where and as the hand model was, and eases from that turn and size into the arc's. The two are one piece: a weapon's, a shield's, and a focus's ground model is their hand model's vertices laid down by one transform, which the scene finds by matching the two models vertex by vertex (`laidDown` in `lootMarkers.ts`), so one item leaves the hand with no jump. A pair that does not match flies from the hand's bone, as an item no hand draws does. A `held` with no thrown `loot` before it empties the hand at once. Under reduced motion, and in a tab stalled as the combat effects judge it (`CombatEffects.stalled`), it lies where it fell at once and the hand empties with the `held`, and one in the air lies there when a stall ends, a hand it had not left yet emptied then. The flight moves the model alone, through a node over its holder, and the drop's bounds are always measured where it lies, so `window.somnio.loot()` points at where it lands throughout.

The markers come from a pool of 16 (`LOOT_MARKER_POOL_SIZE`), which draws the drops nearest the player, the older first of two as near. A drop lies as long as the server says, so with more lying than that the further ones wait without a marker. The pool chooses again as a drop comes or goes, and on any frame a waiting drop lies more than 0.25 m nearer the player than a drawn drop on the ground (`RECHOOSE_MARGIN_METRES`), a drop that has just landed included. So a drop that comes near, or one waiting as a far drop lands, takes the marker of one now among the furthest and lies there without a flight, while two drops about as far do not trade it as the player shifts. A drop in the air keeps its marker until it lands. The name's, the glow's, and the beam's materials compile in `WorldScene.prewarm`, the models draw with the pack's own materials as the props do, and none adds a light.

A press on a drop is a walk with an arrival: `GameplayPredictor.walkTo(point, {within, act})` walks an ordinary path and runs `act` on the first tick the player stands within `within` of the point, short of it or where a path round a body standing on it stops, after `flush()`, at most once. A walk that ends out of range, or that a correction, a fall, a door, an overlay, or a new walk ends first, never acts, and a press on the ground drops the arrival from the walk it lets go on. The pick-up asks only for a drop still lying there and into a bag with room; a full bag is said in chat instead.

**Speech.** `clientSay {text, kind}` says a line as a `whisper`, `say`, or `yell`. Each kind is heard whole within its clear radius, crumbles across a band out to its reach, and is not heard past it (`SPEECH` in `packages/core/src/speech.ts`: 1.5 / 3 m, 6 / 12 m, 40 / 80 m). Sector interest plays no part. Every other player in the space within reach gets `serverSay {entityId, name, kind, text, clarity, x, z}`, and the speaker's own client draws the line itself. The server crumbles the text at each listener's clarity, so no client can undo it. In the band at least one word is always missing, each run of missed words reads `...`, and `clarity` falls from 1 toward 0. Every listener's crumble of one line draws on the same rolls, so a listener further off misses every word a nearer one missed, and listeners who compare notes learn little more than the nearest heard. `x`/`z` is where the voice comes from in the listener's space. An NPC's lines follow the `say` rule from where it stands. The dreamer it addresses stands within `speakingDistance`, inside the clear radius, so they always hear its lines whole.

An NPC's line reaches each listener in the `locale` (`en` or `de`) their `login` or `redeemSession` named, or in English when it named none. The connection keeps the locale until it closes, and nothing saves it. The server renders the line in that language before it crumbles it. The rolls are shared across languages, but a further listener missing every word a nearer one missed holds only within one language: an English and a German listener of one NPC line can piece together more than either heard. Player speech is never translated.

A yell also goes one door further, into the space beyond a live door. A listener there hears it from the counterpart's doorway, by the shortest way through. That way runs from the yeller to the door, plus `doorMuffleMetres`, plus from the doorway to the listener. The muffle is larger than a yell's clear radius, so a voice through a door always crumbles. It is not relayed again. `entityId` names the yeller even there, so a client whose bubble for them is placed by the door hangs it over their head once they come through.

**Pools and condition.** The rules and every number are in `packages/core/src/combat.ts`, shared by both sides. `energy` carries a player's exact pools, as whole numbers, to that player alone, on every change. Their maxima come from the player's `Stats` (`maxPools`, see "Gear"): Health from Toughening and gear, Balance from gear, and Spirit from the Spirit deepening of the player's role and gear. Everyone in view learns another entity's health as its `condition`, and only the player who targets it learns more, from `vitals`. The condition is `hale` only at full health, then `grazed`, `wounded`, `hurt`, and `failing` by quarters of the maximum, and `fallen` at none. Each `entity` frame carries it, and `condition {entityId, condition}` goes out only when the band changes. `blow {attackerId, targetId, hit}` announces each swing and each strike. Balance drains by the distance run and recovers on the server's clock by the gait last reported. Winded is never sent: server and client each start it from the balance at the join and fold the same rule over the same `energy` frames. The server does not shorten a winded player's allowance, so an honest client is still never corrected for it.

**Falling, raising, waking.** A nightmare's strike or a boss's wail that takes the last of a player's health is the only way to fall. The fall comes with a `correction`, because the client may have predicted a little past the last accepted position. A fallen player's moves are answered with `correction`, their `interact`, service, and `pickUp` requests and an `engage {on: true}` are dropped, and they can still choose a target, give up a quest, and speak, a yell included.

`raising {targetId, healerIds, state, progress, seconds, pausedSeconds?}` is a whole snapshot of a raise. It goes to everyone who sees the fallen player's sector when the raise begins, when its Healers change, when a channel of one of them starts or ends, and when it ends, and to a player who comes into view of one under way. While `holding` it names the Healers holding on, those engaged on the fallen player within touch, in the order they took hold, the share done as of the moment it is sent, and the seconds left at their pace. A Healer holds on through a channel of theirs but adds their pace only from its end, so the seconds count each Healer from when their channel ends. `pausedSeconds` says when that is: the seconds before each Healer named adds their pace, in the order of `healerIds`. It is left out while every one adds it now. Both sides pace a raise by one rule, `raiseWorkOver` in `packages/core/src/combat.ts`, with `raiseSecondsFor` its inverse. The server reads the raise's work and each snapshot's seconds left from it. The client reads the share done from it (`raiseShownAt` in `packages/web/src/client/renderSurface.ts`) for the frames' bar and the world's ring alike, counted from the moment the snapshot arrived. So both hold while every Healer's pace waits, and neither runs ahead of the next snapshot except by that frame's extra delay on the network.

The server brings a raise up to now at the start and the end of each step. Between steps it also does so as a channel starts or breaks and as a Healer leaves, so those count from the moment they happen. Every snapshot reads the raise up to the moment it is sent. A Healer who drops out any other way is counted until the step that finds them out. A raise therefore takes its whole time from the step it begins in, and one whose time is up drops no one before the step's end completes it. `done` and `broken` name those holding on just before the end.

Each Healer holding on adds a pace, so two finish the raise in half the time. Each pays spirit for the seconds they are counted, and one who leaves pays before they go. A Healer drops out when they switch slot 1 off or target another, move out of touch, put the Moonstone away, run out of spirit, fall, or leave. That takes away only their pace, and the raise goes on from where it stands. It breaks, and starts over from nothing, only when the last of them drops out. Taking hold, to begin or to join, spends the Healer's touch, as a mend does, so a Healer stepping in and out of reach takes hold at most once a touch interval while they stay in the space. Their Moonstone reaches no one for that interval. Each Healer who held on and is still in the space when it is done earns their share of the practice, by the seconds they held.

`wake {}` from a fallen player transfers them to the wake-point: the `spawn` of the `EdariaInn` sector, or the starter spawn when that sector is missing or has no `spawn`, which the server logs at boot.

**Fixtures and services.** A service carrier is an NPC whose record names a `service`, or a fixture, a sector record that attaches a service to one of the sector's placements (see "Sector format"). `SERVICES` holds the three masters (`fighterMaster`, `healerMaster`, `mysticMaster`), the `shopkeeper`, and the `bountyBoard`. A carrier named as a giver or a turn-in in `packages/core/data/quests.json` carries the quests besides (`carriesQuests`), with no record of its own, so Libus and Memor, who carry no service, open the panel as a master does. A fixture is never an entity: it has no condition, plaque, or Players entry and cannot be targeted, and `ENTITY_KINDS` knows nothing of it. It goes by `fixture:<sector>/<id>` (`fixtureCarrierId`), as an NPC goes by `npc:<sector>/<id>` (`npcEntityId`), and `maxSectorNameUTF8Bytes` keeps the longer of the two within `maxEntityIdUTF8Bytes` at a record id's cap. It stands at its placement's centre (`placedFixtures` in `packages/core/src/sector.ts`).

One gate serves every request to a carrier, `interact`, `acceptQuest`, `turnInQuest`, `study`, `buy`, and `sell` alike: a standing player within speaking distance (`speakingDistance`) of the NPC or of the fixture's placement centre (`withinSpeakingDistance`). Anything else is dropped silently. The client learns the fixtures from the `sector` frames and an NPC's service from its `entity` frame, and walks to a fixture with an arrival (`GameplayPredictor.walkTo` with `within` the speaking distance), which sends `interact` after `flush()` once the player stands there. The path's goal lies inside the fixture's collider, so the walk ends short of it, within that distance.

**Lucidity.** `lucidity {role?, ranks, study?}` is what a player has grown into, sent to them alone at each join and on every change. The rules are in `packages/core/src/lucidity.ts`. `study {carrierId, teachingId}` asks a master of the player's role to set them to study a teaching. The masters are Pugnax (`fighterMaster`), Sana (`healerMaster`), and Tacita (`mysticMaster`). Each master's trial (`TRIAL_QUESTS`) and the task that gates a teaching of theirs (`Teaching.gate`) are quests the master gives and takes (see "Quests"): the trial to a player with no role, and a gate to a player of the master's role whose teaching waits on it. A trial's turn-in, taken only while the player still has no role, makes the role theirs for good and teaches its first teaching, which it sets them to study. The Healer's trial also gives the Moonstone, which comes from nowhere else, and the Mystic's a wand, plain, into an empty off hand, else into the bag (`TRIAL_REWARDS`, and `grantPlace` and `grantItem` in core). A gate's turn-in teaches the first rank of its teaching.

The Mystics' trial is three `reach` objectives: three hidden places, each a point and a radius in a named sector that no map marks, found by standing within it once, in any order, and then a return to Tacita, whose dialog hints at them. The space checks every accepted `move` against each `reach` objective a player holds (`reachedAt`). One line says all three (`objectiveLines`), so the Quest log and Tacita's panel count the places found, never which, and the client says in chat when a place is found, unless that one finishes the trial.

**Quests.** The quests are `QUESTS` in `packages/core/src/quests.ts`, read from `packages/core/data/quests.json` by kebab-case id. A quest names its giver and its turn-in, each a carrier id or a service, and is offered and turned in only at a carrier so named (`questCarriedBy`), on both sides. A service stands for every carrier of it, as `bountyBoard` gives and takes every bounty. It may need a role or another quest turned in. It may forbid striking (`withoutStriking`, as the Healers' trial does): a player holding it cannot swing (`forbidsStriking`, which `swingAllowed` and `engageAction` read). It may start over on a fall (`withoutFalling`). Its reward is coins, practice toward the teaching studied, and an item rolled at a tier (`rollQuestGear` in `packages/core/src/loot.ts`: magic or rare, of a base the player's role can use). Its objectives are counted in order, each toward its goal (`objectiveGoal`):

- `driveOff {count, monsterKind?, sector?}`, nightmares driven off, credited to every sharer as the practice is;
- `collect {monsterKind, count}`, the kind's trophies picked up (see "Drops");
- `visit {carrier}`, an `interact`, `acceptQuest`, or `turnInQuest` at the carrier, which covers reading the bounty board;
- `reach {sector, place?}`, standing within the place, sector-relative, or anywhere in the sector without one;
- `mend {amount}`, health mended on other dreamers.

`SpaceActor.advanceQuests` is the one sink of progress, over `advancedQuests` in core. A player holds at most `maxActiveQuests` (10) and keeps the ids of the quests turned in, which are never offered again. `acceptQuest {carrierId, questId}` takes one on at its giver, by `mayAccept`: one neither held nor turned in, with room in the log and its needs met, and a trial only for a player with no role who holds no other trial, so the Healers' trial's `withoutStriking` never blocks the swing the Fighters' needs. A bounty asks only room in the log and that it is neither held nor turned in (`mayTakeBounty`). Nothing is granted on taking a quest on.

`turnInQuest {carrierId, questId}` at its turn-in pays a quest whose every objective is met (`turnInFor`). While an item it gives, a trial's or a reward's, has nowhere to go, nothing is granted, not the role, the teaching, the coins, or the practice, and the quest waits, done, until there is room. A turn-in that gives items is answered with one `inventory` frame carrying `rewarded`, each item's contents as a drop's (`{kind: 'item', baseId, rarity}`), so the coins and the items arrive together. The client announces the coins gained and then each item, as it does a pick-up, and the banner over the world says the whole turn-in (see "The quest tracker, the line, and the banner"). `abandonQuest {questId}` gives one up anywhere, and what was done toward it is gone. A refusal of any of them is dropped silently.

`quests {active, done, bounties}` goes to the player alone at each join and on every change. `active` holds each quest held as `{questId, progress, definition?}`, `progress` a count for each objective and `definition` a bounty's, `done` the ids turned in, and `bounties` the world day's. `questsMessage` and `questsOf` turn a `QuestLog` into the frame and back, and the client keeps only the quests it knows. The server saves the log with each checkpoint, as `character_quests` rows in their order, and loads it as the player joins. A row it cannot read refuses the join with `characterResult failure`.

**The bounty board.** `packages/core/src/bounties.ts` sets each world day's bounties (`worldDay` in `packages/core/src/worldClock.ts`). There is one for each land: a sector of any space whose monster spawns keep a kind that is no boss (`bountyLands`), so the crypt is a land and the burial chamber is not. Each rolls one of its land's kinds and an objective of `BOUNTY_OBJECTIVES`, `driveOff` or `collect`, from the day and the land's name alone, so every dreamer is offered the same. It asks twice as many as the land keeps alive of the kind, at most 10, and pays the kind's bounty in coins for each it asks and an item at the highest tier among the land's kinds (`dayBounties`). A drive-off counts only in the land, and a trophy wherever its kind left it. The server generates them from its sectors, since a `sector` frame carries no spawns, and every `quests` frame carries the day's. As the world clock enters a new day (`WorldClockService.onNewDay`), every space reaches it and sends each player there a new `quests` frame, without the bounties turned in on another day, which block nothing. Joining a space drops those too. A bounty's quest id names its day and what it asks, and taking one on stores its definition with it, so it is finished and paid as it was offered after the board turns, a reconnect, a restart, or a change to the spawns. The board is the notice board on Edaria's square, the fixture `fixture:EdariaSquare/fixture-1`.

## Sector format

Sectors are JSON, stored in `.somnio-sector` files, read and written by `packages/core/src/sectorFile.ts`. The writer produces plain JSON with a 2-space indent, recursively sorted keys, and a trailing newline, and leaves defaults out, so committed fixtures stay human-diffable and byte-stable across an unedited open-and-save. The reader checks the file's byte size before parsing (`maxSectorFileBytes`) and bounds the sector's extent, every coordinate, and every record-array count (`SOMNIO_PROTOCOL_CONSTANTS` for the view, `SOMNIO_CONSTANTS` for NPCs and monster spawns), so a hostile `.somnio-sector` cannot drive an unbounded allocation when loaded from `SOMNIO_SECTORS_DIR`. The reader's check runs on text already in memory, so the server's loader refuses a file over that size before reading it. It also refuses anything but a regular file, since a FIFO or a device reports size 0 and reads without end.

A sector file is the **sector view** plus the content only the server acts on. The view (`SectorView` in `packages/protocol/src/sectorView.ts`) is declared once and is exactly what a `sector` frame carries as its `sector`; `decodeSectorView` validates it for both. Beside it, an interior's frame carries `outdoorDoorIds`, the ids of its doors whose counterpart lies outdoors, which the server derives from the world and no file holds. Property names are the JSON keys, so renaming one changes the file and the wire. The sector's `name` is the filename stem (at most `maxSectorNameUTF8Bytes`, so an NPC's entity id and a fixture's carrier id always fit their cap) and never part of the file: the reader injects it and the writer strips it. One record of each kind, in reading order (the file sorts its keys):

```json
{
  "kind": "outdoor",
  "origin": { "x": 0, "z": 0 },
  "size": { "width": 40.96, "depth": 40.96 },
  "ground": {
    "backgroundMaterialId": "grass-meadow",
    "marks": [
      {
        "id": "plaza",
        "materialId": "cobble-town",
        "shape": {
          "kind": "polygon",
          "points": [
            { "x": 16, "z": 0 },
            { "x": 24.96, "z": 0 },
            { "x": 24.96, "z": 8 }
          ]
        }
      },
      {
        "id": "road",
        "materialId": "dirt-path",
        "opacity": 0.8,
        "shape": {
          "kind": "stroke",
          "radius": 1.6,
          "points": [
            { "x": 2, "z": 20 },
            { "x": 38, "z": 22 }
          ]
        }
      }
    ]
  },
  "placements": [
    { "id": "building-townhall-1", "modelId": "building-townhall", "x": 36.8, "z": 4.48, "yaw": 270 },
    { "id": "notice-board-1", "modelId": "notice-board", "x": 18.15, "z": 18.1, "yaw": 35 }
  ],
  "blockers": [{ "id": "blocker-1", "x": 0, "z": 0, "width": 5.12, "depth": 0.44 }],
  "doors": [{ "id": "to-edarialibrary", "placement": "building-townhall-1", "anchor": "main", "target": { "sector": "EdariaLibrary", "door": "exit" } }],
  "fixtures": [{ "id": "fixture-1", "placement": "notice-board-1", "service": "bountyBoard" }],
  "spawn": { "x": 2.88, "z": 4.32, "facing": 0 },
  "npcs": [
    {
      "id": "pugnax",
      "name": "Pugnax",
      "characterModelId": "fighter-master",
      "x": 37.32,
      "z": 33.2,
      "facing": 0,
      "dialogScript": "...",
      "service": "fighterMaster"
    }
  ],
  "monsterSpawns": [{ "id": "spawn-1", "kind": "ghost", "x": 0.64, "z": 1.28, "width": 5.76, "depth": 7.68, "maxAlive": 3 }]
}
```

Lengths are metres, `x` runs east and `z` south, and every record position is relative to the sector's north-west corner. `x`/`z` is the centre of a placement, an NPC, and the spawn, and the north-west corner of a rect (a blocker, a monster spawn's area). Angles are degrees: `yaw` counter-clockwise seen from above, `facing` a heading with 0 = south and 90 = east, normalized on decode.

`kind` is `outdoor` or `interior`. An outdoor sector carries an `origin`, its north-west corner in the one outdoor space, and is lit by the world clock. An interior is a space of its own and carries a `brightness` percentage (0-100) instead, which scales its steady light. Only the light through its first door to the outdoors follows the clock (see "Rooms are lit by their own flames"). Each of the two keys is rejected on the other kind.

Every record has an `id` unique within its array (lowercase letters, digits, and hyphens, at most 64 bytes), and the decoder rejects a duplicate. Ids are what a door, the editor's selection, and persisted NPC dialog progress refer to, so reordering records changes nothing.

- **Placements** put a registry model (`modelId`) at `x`/`z`, turned by `yaw` (models are normalized with their door or long axis on +X, so 270 faces a door south) and lifted by `elevation` (a candle on a table). A placement blocks through its model's registry colliders. `elevation` is visual only, and is ignored on a model with walk surfaces. An unmapped `modelId` renders a placeholder and contributes no collision rather than rejecting the file.
- **Blockers** are invisible blocking rects for what no model covers.
- **The ground** is a `backgroundMaterialId` and `marks` painted over it in array order. A mark is a `stroke` (a round-capped polyline `radius` wide on each side, at most 10 m; one point makes a dab) or a `polygon` (at least three points, filled even-odd), in one `materialId` at an `opacity` from 0 to 1. Every mark's edge fades over `markFeatherMetres`, broken by noise reaching at most `markEdgeNoiseMetres`, so no mark has a hard edge. Its paint may reach at most 1.5 m past the sector's rect, so a mark only ever paints the sector and those touching it. `markPointBounds` in `packages/protocol/src/sectorView.ts` is the rect its points must lie in, and the decoder refuses a point outside it. A sector holds at most 512 marks and 8,192 points in all, both counted before any mark is decoded. Marks are purely visual, and a material the client has no recipe for is skipped (a background without one draws the first recipe).
- **Doors** are attached to a placement's door anchor (`placement` + `anchor`, the anchor being one of the model's registry `doors`) and name their counterpart (`target.sector` + `target.door`). The decoder rejects a door whose `placement` is not in the sector. The trigger is the anchor's width by `doorTriggerDepth` in front of it, and a player arrives 0.8 m out from the counterpart door, facing away from it. An optional `nightOnly: true` opens the door only while the sun is down (see "Doors" under "Wire protocol"). It is part of the view, so the client knows it too. Put it on the outer half of a pair only, as on the tower's door in `EdariaSquare`, so no dreamer is ever shut in.
- **Fixtures** attach a `service` of `SERVICES` to a placement of the sector (`placement`), as the bounty board's notice board on `EdariaSquare` carries `bountyBoard` (see "Fixtures and services"). The decoder rejects a fixture whose `placement` is not in the sector, as it does a door's. A fixture is part of the view, so the client learns it from the `sector` frame and draws nothing beyond its placement.
- **`spawn`** is where a new character enters the world, in the starter sector, and where a dreamer who gave up wakes, in the inn. The starter sector must have one.
- **NPCs** stand at `x`/`z`, drawn with a registry character model (`characterModelId`), and speak the steps of their `dialogScript`: the first as a greeting to whoever comes near, the rest when asked. An optional `service` of `SERVICES` makes the NPC a master (`fighterMaster`, `healerMaster`, `mysticMaster`) or the shopkeeper (`shopkeeper`), whose panel opens when it is asked. An NPC a quest names as its giver or turn-in carries the quests without one. It reaches the client on the NPC's `entity` frame. The reader rejects an NPC standing outside its own sector's rect, because players are told about an NPC by the sector that owns it. It also rejects a step that could pass the say cap once each `$name` becomes the longest character name, because a client closes on an over-cap line.
- **Monster spawns** are areas that keep up to `maxAlive` monsters (at most `maxSpawnAlive`) of one `kind` from `packages/core/src/monsterKinds.ts` alive. The area is also its monsters' home ground, which they never leave, so where it ends is where a dreamer can retreat to. A monster chases only a dreamer standing inside it, the foremost by threat (see "Threat"), and walks back to where it spawned when there is none. It strikes any standing dreamer within its reach, just outside the area too, so the edge is no line to strike from unanswered. Harm from afar by a dreamer standing outside the area, beyond that answer, it evades (see Dream-bolt). An optional `eliteChance`, from 0 to 1, is the chance that a monster it spawns is an elite (see "Elites"); the editor's monster-spawn inspector edits it as Elite chance. A spawn of a boss's kind spawns a boss instead, whatever its `eliteChance` (see "Bosses"), and its area is the boss's chamber, which its minions keep to as well. The Ghost is of the first ring, and the Skeleton, the Skeleton Warrior, and the Necromancer of the third (`MONSTER_KINDS`).

The `spawn`, the NPCs, and the monster spawns never reach a client as records: `sectorView` strips the server-side content, and NPCs and monsters arrive as entities. The bounties the spawns set reach the client in the `quests` frame instead (see "The bounty board").

Omitted on write and defaulted on read: `yaw` and `elevation` at 0, a mark's `opacity` at 1, empty record arrays and marks, an unset `spawn`, a door's `nightOnly` at false, a monster spawn's `eliteChance` at 0, and an NPC's unset `service`.

The server builds the world from the loaded sectors at boot (`buildWorld` in `packages/core/src/world.ts`). Every outdoor sector lies in the one space `outdoors` at its origin, so sectors that touch are walked across with no transition and a sector edge with no neighbour blocks. Each interior is a space named after its sector, reached through a door. Overlapping outdoor sectors, an outdoor sector under `minOutdoorSectorExtent`, a sector named `outdoors`, a spawn a body cannot stand on, and a starter sector without a spawn fail startup.

Two outdoor sectors that do not touch must stand at least `SECTOR_BORDER_SUPPORT_METRES` (4 m) apart, measured from each one's padded raster rect (its rect plus the ground's gutter, snapped to the texel grid) to the other's rect. A client is sent only the sectors touching the player's, so it can blend a sector's ground only with those, and a sector closer than that would paint into a border it is not part of. A pair that breaks this is logged under each of the two, as a `sector` record naming the other, and the world still loads with an uneven seam there. The numbers are in `packages/core/src/ground.ts`, and the check is `spacingIssues` in `packages/core/src/world.ts`.

A door is live only as half of a sound pair: both doors resolve to an anchor, point at each other, and have an arrival point a body can stand on. Any other door is logged and left inert, and the world still loads. Standing means clear of colliders, blockers, ledges, and the space's NPCs, because a body arriving in contact with an NPC could never move again. An unmapped model, an `elevation` on a model with walk surfaces, and walk surfaces of two placements that overlap are logged the same way. A live door whose arrival point lies within a nightmare's reach of a monster spawn's area is logged too, because a dreamer arriving there can be struck before they see the room. It stays live, since the pair itself is sound.

The outdoor lands' dressing and roads are composed by a seeded generator, `packages/core/scripts/compose/` (run by hand: `node packages/core/scripts/compose/main.ts`). `rules.ts` holds each land's roads, clearings, worn ground, density, model mixes, and seed, and mirrors the splash reel's outdoor paths as keep-outs. The generator routes a dirt path from each outdoor door to the nearest road, which in the town is a street. `rules.ts` also lists the town's compound openings that no door path runs through, each of which gets a path of its own, and the compounds whose two door paths do not meet, which get a path joining them. The generator keeps each model's crown, the circle round its footprint, off every route and keep-out, so no canopy hangs over a path or a splash figure. Over 3 m beside them it thins the dressing from sparse to full, so a route does not run between hard walls of trees. `splash-reel.test.ts` proves the reel's paths legal against the composed lands, so the keep-outs and the reel move together. Every record it writes has an id starting `gen-`. One run strips those from every outdoor sector and composes them again in name order, so composing its own output writes the same bytes. Every other record (the town's walls and buildings, doors, NPCs, a mark painted by hand) is kept as it is, and hand marks paint after the generated ones.

The rooms' walls are ordinary placements of the stone and plaster-and-timber wall sets, with their inner faces on the sector's edge. The walls are full on the north and west edges and low on the south and east, because the camera looks in from the south-east. Each room chooses which sections of its back walls are windows, and keeps anything tall or wall-hung off those sections. Each door is a low threshold of its wall set (`timber-room-threshold`, `stone-room-threshold`) lying across a two-module gap. Its anchor is on the wall's inner face, and a mat lies just inside. A room's floor is its background material. A boarded floor within it (the Fighter Hall's sparring floor, the Arena's refuge, the strip by its door beyond every ghost's reach) is laid from `floor-boards` tiles, one module each, because a painted mark would blend at its edge.

The canonical `.somnio-sector` extension is used everywhere: the committed fixtures (`packages/core/fixtures/sectors`), the server's `SOMNIO_SECTORS_DIR`, and the web editor's file API. The server loads only `.somnio-sector` files and keys each by its extension-stripped filename (the filename-as-sector-id convention door targets rely on); a directory with no `.somnio-sector` files fails startup (`noSectorsLoaded`) rather than booting an empty world.

## Model registry

The 3D pack's layout and its collision geometry are data: the committed `packages/core/data/ModelRegistry.json` (exported as `@somnio/core/data/ModelRegistry.json`) holds `characterModels` (character model ids to model stems, each with its `expectedClips` clip-presence contract), `playerModel` (the one character model every player uses), `handModels` (the ids of the base items a hand holds to the stems drawn in that hand, from the `held` of a dreamer's `entity` and `held` frames), `groundModels` (the ids of the base items that can drop to the stems a drop lies on the ground as, every base but the Moonstone), and `objectModels` (semantic object ids to prop stems and their geometry). The player's model and each nightmare also expect the clips that play once: the fall, a swing, and a flinch, the player's model the throw and the abilities' clips besides, and the Necromancer's its long cast and its summon (see "One-shot clips have no fallback"). The registry references only filename stems, so it never drifts from the uncommitted, operator-supplied model pack.

An object model's geometry is in model space: metres, the origin at the ground-footprint centre, +X east and +Z south at yaw 0, a rect's `x`/`z` its minimum (north-west) corner. A placement is tested in its model's space, so axis-aligned registry rects work at any yaw.

- `footprint` is the measured ground bounds, centred on the origin.
- `colliders` are the rects that block. Left out, they default to the footprint; `[]` never blocks (`rug`, `candle`, `door-mat`, the wall-hung pieces).
- `walkSurfaces` are rects with one `height` each, a stair run being one rect per tread. A body steps between two heights up to `maxStepHeight` apart (the ground is 0), and every stretch of a surface edge with a larger drop is a blocking ledge.
- `doors` are the anchors a sector's door records attach to: `id`, `x`, `z`, a cardinal `facing` out of the door, and the opening's `width`.
- `light` is the flame a model carries: a point in model space, `y` above its ground, and a `kind` from `LIGHT_KINDS` (`candle`, `hearth`, `lamp`, `torch`) that the client lights by its own values. Every placement of the model in a room is lit, so an unlit variant needs a model of its own.

`packages/core/src/modelRegistry.ts` validates the structural invariants on read: non-empty stems and ids, no duplicate ids, characters expecting at least one clip, a `playerModel` that is a character model, hand and ground models of known base items, positive sizes and heights, cardinal door facings, walk surfaces of one model that do not overlap (steps cut into a plinth would otherwise resolve to the plinth's height), and lights of a known kind not below the ground. The browser degrades to a placeholder registry with a logged error; the server parses the same file strictly at boot and fails startup on an error, because an empty registry there is a world with no collision and every door inert.

The web editor's model, character, and door-anchor pickers are populated from the same registry, so the authoring surface can only reference what resolves. The asset pipeline's clip-presence, footprint, hand-prop, and ground-item gates read the same file, and its `Pipeline/glb_surface_report.py` is where a model's footprint, colliders, walk surfaces, and door anchors are measured.
