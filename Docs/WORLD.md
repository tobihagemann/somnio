# Somnio — World & Design Canon

The design bible for Somnio's world: its premise, peoples, classes, and the loop they live in. The source of truth for what the world *is* and why.

Somnio plays from Diablo's fixed overhead view, by tap and click, with an MMO's structure (an ability bar, threat, a target frame, quests, several characters) and an engine that keeps making play (random gear, elite nightmares, repeatable bounties). Clan Lord's role play fills the rest: speech by distance, falling and being raised by Healers, peoples, masters, and an open world gated only by its nightmares. One person builds the world, so systems that keep producing play win over hand-made content.

A part marked *(being built)* is designed but not yet in the world.

## Design ethos

Somnio is **cooperative and community-first** — the world pushes players together, not against each other:

- **Support roles matter.** Healer and Mystic cannot go it alone; they exist to keep a group alive and aware. That interdependence is the point — you *need* other people.
- **The threat is the world, not each other.** No player-versus-player harm: nothing a dreamer does hurts another.
- **No party, no instance.** Dreamers group by standing together. Every standing dreamer nearby shares the credit and the coins for a nightmare driven off, and so does the one who drove it off, from however far. Every place, the lairs included, belongs to whoever walks in.
- **Progression is shared purpose.** Everyone is defending the same dream.

## Premise — the dream of a restless Sleeper

**Somnio** is Latin for *"I dream."*

> The whole world is the dream of a single great **Sleeper** — one mind's dream, in its 500th year. Dreamers live inside it: the human-like **Vigils** and the dream-born peoples. They enter Somnio through the **Library** in Edaria, the threshold where sleeping minds arrive.
>
> But the Sleeper is growing **restless**. As they stir toward waking, the dream frays at its edges, and **nightmares seep through the cracks** — that is what the monsters *are*. Edaria is the lucid heart, the calmest place, farthest from the unraveling verge. The dreamers' shared purpose: venture to the fraying edges, push the nightmares back, and **hold the dream together** — because if the Sleeper ever fully wakes, Somnio ends.

This single mechanism explains everything else:

- **Monsters = nightmares**, symptoms of the Sleeper's unrest.
- **Death = waking** — a fallen dreamer briefly slips out of the dream and must be drawn back (see *Death*).
- **Cooperation** — you are all defending the one shared dream.
- **Progression = lucidity** — mastering the dream lets you walk deeper toward the fraying edge without coming apart.
- **Edaria & the Library matter** — the lucid heart and the gate.

(Endgame lore deliberately left open: *who* is the Sleeper, and whether they can ever be soothed rather than merely held back.)

## Tone

**Dream-tinged fantasy.** Mostly grounded medieval-fantasy — knights, libraries, ghosts, taverns, a town you know. Dream-logic lives at the *edges*: the further from Edaria, the stranger and more oneiric the world becomes. The dream is flavor and structure, not constant surrealism.

## The loop

A dreamer wakes into the Library, finds their feet in Edaria, then ventures outward to where the dream frays. They face nightmares, grow in lucidity, and return to Edaria's masters and shops before going deeper — always holding the line against the unraveling, always needing others to do it.

Between the masters' trials and teachings, the work never runs out. Nightmares drop gear, rolled stronger the further out they fall. Quest givers send a dreamer on errands and chains, the bounty board on the square posts fresh bounties every world day, and elites and lairs wait for dreamers who come together. *(being built)*

## Moving and acting

The view looks down from a fixed angle at about fourteen metres of ground, top to bottom. Desktop and landscape phone share one way to play, and an upright phone asks to be turned.

- **Walking.** A tap or click on the ground walks the dreamer there, along a path round walls, props, and whoever stands in the way. Holding steers toward the pointer, and letting go walks on to where it was let go. A tap on the dreamer's own body walks to the ground under it, as a tap anywhere else does. Where the tapped spot cannot be reached, the dreamer walks to the nearest point toward it.
- **Facing.** On a desktop the dreamer faces the cursor, or the target a right press locks their gaze on, so they can back away from a nightmare while still facing it, slower sideways and slower still backwards. A phone has no cursor, so there the dreamer faces their target, or the way they walk without one.
- **Pace.** A dreamer jogs. The Run toggle, on the bar or Shift on a desktop, makes them run, which drains Balance. A winded dreamer walks.
- **Targets.** A tap on anyone or anything alive makes it the dreamer's target and never walks them, as does a tap on a name in the Players list, and a tap on the dreamer's own frame targets themselves. A frame shows the target's name and a bar of its health. There are no damage numbers.
- **Slot 1.** The first button on the bar is a toggle like Run, and acts on the target. At a nightmare it is **Attack**, with the main hand empty or holding a weapon: it swings on the swing rhythm at that target alone while it stands in reach in front of the dreamer. At a dreamer, or at themselves, it is **Mend** for a Healer with the Moonstone in the off hand. At anything else it cannot be switched on. It stays on from one target it can act on to the next, and switches off when the target is lost, when the dreamer falls, and through a door.
- **Abilities.** The bar holds six buttons, keys 1 to 6 on a desktop: slot 1, then the abilities the dreamer's teachings have given them, in the order they were taught. Each costs Balance or Spirit and needs a while before it can be used again, shown as a sweep across its button. Any use holds every ability back for a moment. An ability for a friend lands on the dreamer targeted, or on its user with no dreamer targeted, but never on a fallen dreamer or one too far away. What an ability leaves behind, such as a Brace or a Ward, shows on the frame of whoever holds it, and on the target frame of whoever targets them. Most act at once. Deep mend is a short channel, shown as a bar draining under the frame, that breaks if the Healer moves; slot 1 waits while it runs.
- **People.** Someone a dreamer comes near greets them unasked. A tap from within speaking distance asks them to go on and opens a master's or the shopkeeper's offer. A quest giver shows a marker over their head *(being built)*.
- **Speech.** Words carry by distance. A whisper reaches only those close by, a plain line a little further, and a yell far, even one door into the next room. Further off, a line arrives in pieces.

Walking into someone only stops a dreamer.

## The classes — three roles that need each other

Three roles in tension, where support classes *cannot* go it alone. A class is earned by passing its master's trial and is kept for good.

- **Fighter** (Balance) — the front line. Holds nightmares on themselves and strikes them down. The only truly self-sufficient role — but bring friends. Each of their abilities spends Balance and is used with the main hand empty or holding a weapon. **Cleave** swings at every nightmare in front within touch. **Challenge** turns every nightmare a few steps around the Fighter on them for a few breaths. **Brace** halves the harm they take for a few seconds, and they cannot run while it holds. **Lunge** leaps to the targeted nightmare from a few steps off. **Rally** is a shout that gives Balance back to every standing dreamer who would hear a plain line.
- **Healer** (Spirit) — carries the **Moonstone**: heals their target, from a few steps away, draining their own Spirit to do it. Can barely fight. Exists to keep the group breathing. Precious and rare. **Touch** and **Drawing back** happen while **Mend** is on: the Moonstone mends the targeted dreamer, the Healer included, once a second, and waits while Spirit is short. A fallen dreamer is drawn back only by touch, and several Healers with Mend on at one fallen dreamer draw them back together. Each of their abilities spends Spirit and needs the Moonstone in the off hand. **Deep mend** mends deeply after a short channel, on the targeted dreamer or the Healer themselves, and moving breaks it off. **Renewal** mends a little every second for a while. **Radiance** mends every dreamer close by, the Healer included. **Calm** makes nightmares forget half their anger at the Healer, and for a while the Healer stirs them only half as much. Nothing a Healer does mends the fallen; only drawing back stands them up.
- **Mystic** (Spirit) — *deliberately secretive* support, not a fireball-wizard. Wards allies, weakens and binds nightmares, **locates the fallen**, and senses the dream's hidden currents — invaluable in a world where nightmares bleed in unseen. Each of their abilities spends Spirit and needs a focus, such as a wand, in the off hand. **Ward** shields the targeted dreamer, or the Mystic, taking the harm of the next blows until it breaks or fades. **Weaken** softens a nightmare's strikes. **Bind** holds a nightmare in place, though it still strikes whoever stands in reach. **Dream-bolt** is a modest strike from afar that never misses, though a nightmare evades one cast from outside the ground it keeps to. **Veil** hides the dreamers near the Mystic from every nightmare for a few breaths, and the nightmares forget them. A Weaken or a Bind draws the nightmare's eye to the Mystic. **Sense the fallen** needs no button: a Mystic who has learned it always sees, at the edge of their view, which way each fallen dreamer lies, however far off.

**Threat.** A nightmare attacks the dreamer it holds the most threat against: harm done to it, and mending done to the dreamers it fights. With no one on its mind it goes for the nearest, and it forgets whoever falls or leaves its ground. A Fighter's Challenge puts them on top. A Healer's Calm brings them down, and a Mystic's Veil takes a group off the table.

## The peoples — kinds of dreamers

Somnio's races are **kinds of dreamers**, each born in a different stratum of the dream. The launch pantheon is **four**. Class aptitudes are **leanings, not locks** — an unusual pairing is just harder, and wonderful.

| People | Root | What they are | Temperament | Aptitude lean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vigils** (the Waking) | *vigilia* | Newly-arrived sleepers, still half-remembering the waking world. The everyman — and *you*, fresh through the Library gate. | curious, adaptable | none — jack-of-all |
| **Sopors** (the Deep) | *sopor*, deep sleep | Drowsy, stone-skinned giants born in the dream's underlevels; move as if underwater, speak rarely. | stoic, immovable | **Fighter** |
| **Umbrae** (the Shadow) | *umbra* | Wispy, half-translucent folk of the shadow-verge where dream thins toward nightmare — bordering the dark without being of it. | quick, wary, perceptive | **Mystic** |
| **Lumina** (the Light) | *lumen* | Soft, radiant folk of the bright high places; born to mend, communal by nature. The Moonstone feels made for their hands. | warm, selfless | **Healer** |

## Naming convention

- **Ancient dream-Latin** for the oldest, deepest things — the world itself (*Somnio*), the peoples (*Vigils*, *Sopors*, *Umbrae*, *Lumina*), primordial sites, and Edaria's old souls (*Libus*, *Pugnax*, *Sana*, *Tacita*, *Mercus*, *Quieta*). Names from before the common tongue.
- **English** for everything dreamers say — roles, abilities, and items (*Fighter*, *Healer*, *Mystic*, *Cleave*, *Ward*, *Moonstone*), the nightmares (*Ghost*, *Necromancer*), *and the lands dreamers themselves named and walk* (*North Wood*, *the Swamp*, *Troll Village*).
- In-world logic: Latin is the old dream-tongue, spoken by the world's bones; English is the common tongue the dreamers brought with them. The contrast is itself lore. (*Edaria* is the inherited name of the heart — older than either.)
- The game reads in English and in German. German translates the common tongue (Fighter is *Kämpfer*, the Moonstone the *Mondstein*, the North Wood the *Nordwald*) and leaves the Latin as it is.

## Progression, death, economy

- **Lucidity, not levels.** Progression is horizontal skill-ranks under a **master** ("lucidity-mentor"), built up slowly by doing rather than gained in a rush. Gear is the other half of a dreamer's strength.
- **Death is a social event, never permanent.** A fallen dreamer is helpless (briefly "waking") until a **Healer** raises them in the field — or they give up and wake at the inn, weakened. No permadeath; falling pulls the community together.
- **Economy** runs through Edaria's shop and the nightmares. A new dreamer starts with little more than a purse of coins and a humble cudgel. Every nightmare driven off pays coins to the dreamers who stood by, and may leave each of them a piece of gear. The **Moonstone** is the Healer's tool, given by Sana with the role and by no one else: it is never sold, bought, or dropped.

### Gear

- **Rarity.** A piece of gear is plain, magic (one or two stats), or rare (three or four). The stats are drawn from the triad: more Health, Balance, or Spirit, harder hits, Guard, a faster swing, quicker recovery, deeper mends, stronger wards. A piece rolls by the ring of the nightmare that dropped it, so gear from further out rolls higher. So far only the Ghost, of the first ring, drops any *(the further rings are being built)*.
- **Ten slots.** Main hand, off hand, head, chest, hands, legs, feet, an amulet at the neck, and two rings. A dreamer wears one piece in each and carries up to 24 more in the bag. Armour mostly toughens a dreamer, with more Health, Guard, and Balance. A weapon or gloves lean to harder hits and a faster swing, and the off hand, an amulet, or a ring to Spirit, quicker recovery, deeper mends, and stronger wards. The Character window (C or I) shows the dreamer's own body with the ten slots round it and the bag's places below, which the dreamer arranges as they like, and a tab of everything their ranks and gear add up to. A piece shows its stats before it is worn or taken off, and can be dragged into a slot, to another place, or out onto the ground. Only what is in the hands shows on the character, to everyone who sees them.
- **Each hand does its own job.** The main hand swings, bare or with a weapon. The off hand holds a shield, the Healer's Moonstone, or the Mystic's wand, which their abilities need; Tacita gives a wand with the role. A Healer with a sword and the Moonstone can strike a nightmare and mend a dreamer, by whom they target.
- **Drops are personal.** When a nightmare is driven off, each dreamer who stood by may see a drop of their own on the ground, visible only to them, with its name in its rarity's colour. A tap on it walks the dreamer there and picks it up, unless the bag is full. A drop lies until its dreamer leaves the world, waiting for them through doors and wakes, and at most fifty lie for one dreamer, the oldest going first. Most drops are something the dreamer's calling can use: a Fighter finds no wand, a Healer, whose off hand holds the Moonstone, neither a wand nor a shield, and a Mystic no shield. About one in five may be anything, to sell or to give away. To give one away, a dreamer throws it from the bag a step away, toward where they point, where anyone nearby sees it for fifteen minutes and the first to reach it picks it up.
- **Mercus** sells plain gear of every kind but the Moonstone, and buys any piece a dreamer carries in the bag but the Moonstone, paying more for a rarer piece and one from further out.

## Progression — Lucidity (in detail)

Somnio commits to the Clan Lord model of growth: **horizontal skill-ranks, no levels**. A dreamer never outgrows the world numerically; they broaden — more ranks, more capabilities, deeper mastery of the few things their class does. And the world is **open by design**: difficulty is **monster-gated, never zone-gated**. There are no level requirements, no rank checks at a border or a door, no "you may not pass" — any dreamer can walk from the Library straight into the North Wood on day one. What stops them is what lives there. *(Hard rule: no sector, door, or region may ever gain a rank or access gate. The nightmares are the barrier.)*

### The trainable triad

Three trained pools, carried from the game's legacy heritage (in German Gestalt, Gleichgewicht, and Geist):

- **Health** — how firmly the dream-self holds together. Others see how a dreamer or a wounded nightmare fares only as a tint on its name, and the target frame as a bar, never as a number. When Health gives out, the dreamer falls (see *Death* below).
- **Balance** — the combat resource: footing, timing, poise. Every swing spends it, and so do running, being struck, and the Fighter's abilities. The less a dreamer has, the easier they are to hit; recovering it is what the pause in a fight *is*. It recovers fastest standing or at the slow gait, slowly at a jog, and not at all at a run. A dreamer whose Balance runs out is **winded**. Until it is partway back, only the slow gait moves them, and it recovers only while they stand. The Fighter's primary pool.
- **Spirit** — the support resource: the reach of a Healer's Moonstone and the depth of a Mystic's senses. Support acts drain Spirit; an empty Spirit is why neither can go it alone.

### Masters and teachings

A master takes on a dreamer who passes their **trial**, and the dreamer then decides whether to take the role, for good. From there a dreamer studies **one teaching at a time**, and what they do out in the world builds practice toward its next rank: a nightmare driven off nearby, Health mended, a fallen dreamer raised. The rank arrives wherever they are when the practice is full, and each rank asks more than the last. The biggest steps are gated by a **task** the master sets. No experience bar and no rank for sale: depth over rush.

**Abilities are teachings.** Learning a teaching's first rank puts its ability on the bar, and each further rank deepens it: Cleave strikes harder, Brace holds longer, Lunge is ready sooner, Rally gives more back, Deep mend mends more and sooner, a Ward holds more, and a Bind holds longer.

The three trials:

- **Pugnax's trial** — drive off a nightmare. He sends a dreamer to the Arena to find one, and one driven off anywhere counts.
- **Sana's trial** — walk to the North Wood and return without striking anything. A dreamer holding it cannot strike.
- **Tacita's trial** — find three hidden places she only hints at, stand in each, and return to her. No map marks them. Her hints point west, east, and north: a ring of open grass far out in the West Meadow, south of the road; a glade just past the gate into the Autumn Wood, north of the road; and open ground in the North Meadow, west of the road. A dreamer hears when they find one, and how many of the three they have, never which.

A rank's mechanical effect is a **combat or support capability** — hit harder, recover Balance faster, heal deeper, sense further.

### What each master teaches

- **Fighter** — Strike (every hit lands harder), Guard (nightmares miss more often), Follow-through (a shorter pause between swings; its task is three nightmares driven off without falling in between), Balance recovery, and Toughening. Then the Fighter's abilities (see *The classes*): Cleave and Lunge build on Strike, Challenge and Brace on Guard, and Rally on Balance recovery.
- **Healer** — Touch (Mend mends the targeted dreamer from about 3 m), Depth (each touch mends more), Drawing back (raising the fallen by touch; its task is Health mended on other dreamers), and Spirit deepening. Then the Healer's abilities (see *The classes*): Deep mend and Calm build on Touch, Renewal on a deeper Touch, and Radiance on Depth.
- **Mystic** — Ward, the first teaching, and Spirit deepening. Then the Mystic's other abilities (see *The classes*) and Sense the fallen: Weaken, Dream-bolt, and Sense the fallen build on Ward, Bind on Weaken, and Veil on Bind. Taught obliquely; half the lesson is finding the tower open.

A Healer can mend themselves with the Moonstone, for half the practice, so a Healer alone still grows.

### Quests and bounties *(being built)*

- **The quest log.** The masters' trials are quests in a quest log, beside every errand a dreamer takes on. A dreamer who can take or hand in a quest sees a marker over the giver's head.
- **The first quests.** Libus gives a newcomer's errand: visit Mercus, then read the bounty board. The priest at the Graveyard's church gives a chain of three: see what stirs among the graves, gather what the skeletons drop, then go down into the crypt and still the Necromancer.
- **The bounty board** stands on Edaria's square and posts new bounties every world day, made from the nightmares of each land: drive off so many of a kind there, or bring back so many of what they drop. A finished bounty pays coins and a piece of gear, and a bounty taken before the board changes can still be finished and paid after.

### Group content *(being built)*

- **Elites.** Stronger nightmares roam the lands among their kin, each with a trait in its name: a *Stony* one shrugs off part of every blow, a *Warded* one sits behind a ward that keeps renewing. They hit harder to bring down and drop better gear.
- **Lairs.** The first lair is the crypt under the Graveyard, full of skeletons that strike up close, with elites among the packs. At its end waits the **Necromancer**. He wails, and the ground round him marks where the wail will land, so a dreamer still inside when it ends is struck; and he raises minions to fight beside him.

### Death as a social event (never permanent)

Faithful to the heritage: when a dreamer's Health gives out they **fall** — a helpless, briefly "waking" body lying where it dropped, still in the world and still in the way, able to speak and to choose a target, and to do nothing else. A yell carries far, which is how the fallen call for help. Nightmares leave the fallen alone. Two ways back:

- **Raised in the field by a Healer**, and by nobody else. Drawing a dreamer back takes time and a good part of a Healer's Spirit, and the Healer has to stay with them. Several Healers can draw a dreamer back together, sooner and sharing the Spirit it costs. It breaks off and starts over only when the last of them parts. The raised dreamer stands up frail, and nightmares leave them be for a few breaths. Falling pulls the community together; a Mystic who senses the fallen is how a rescue party finds them.
- **Giving up** — the dreamer lets go of the venture and wakes at the inn in Edaria, weakened in all three pools and no poorer.

No permadeath, ever.

### Characters

An account holds several characters, each with a name and a people of its own, chosen on a character card after login. An account is an email and a password, and has no name of its own: what the world shows is the dreamer's. A remembered session resumes the character played last.

A character chooses a people, not a class. A dreamer grows into a class by passing that master's trial and accepting, and the other masters then have nothing to teach them.

## Edaria — the lucid heart

Edaria is Somnio's central town: where every stranger arrives, the calmest place, the one spot the dream never frays. It is a **walled square with four gates** — north, south, east, west — each opening onto a different ring of the lands. Within its walls:

- **The central well** — the heart of the heart, a fountain at the crossing of every path.
- **The Library** — the *threshold* where dreamers arrive; every newcomer first opens their eyes here. Its keeper, **Libus** the gatekeeper-librarian, greets each one: *"Greetings! This library is still a work in progress. And running is forbidden in here!"*
- **The three halls** — where the Fighter, Healer, and Mystic masters keep their doors. Pugnax teaches in the Fighters' hall, the old barracks in the south-east quarter. Sana teaches in the Healers' hall, the chapel beside the inn. Tacita teaches in a tower in the south-west quarter, beside the sealed keep. The tower's door opens only at night, from ten in the evening until six in the morning, while the clock shows the moon, and a dreamer who tries it by day is told so. The night is its only lock: the tower opens to every dreamer once it is dark, whatever their ranks. Its door out opens at every hour.
- **The shop & the inn** — a shop to outfit a dreamer; an inn that is the **wake-point**: a dreamer who gives up wakes here.
- **The bounty board** — on the square, where each world day's bounties are posted. *(being built)*
- **The sealed door** — the door of the keep in the south-west quarter, which no living dreamer has opened, and none can say what lies behind it. (The deepest mystery; tied to the Sleeper.)

## The lands of Somnio — a gazetteer

The world radiates from Edaria in rings. The further out, the more the dream has frayed, the stranger the ground, and the stronger the nightmares — until the encircling **sea**, which is not a border but the place where the dreamed land simply *gives out*. Three of the four town gates open onto a near meadow; the **east gate opens straight into the Autumn Wood** — there is no east meadow, the great wood grows right up to the wall. Everything else lies beyond.

**How the lands connect.** The outdoors is one continuous ground. Each land is a sector (a `.somnio-sector` file; the filename stem is the sector's name) placed by its origin, and where two sectors touch a dreamer walks straight across and sees the next land before reaching it. Interiors — the Library, the shop, the inn, the Arena, the two halls, and Tacita's tower — are rooms of their own. Each is reached through a door on a building and left through the door inside, which sets the dreamer down in front of the one they came in by. The rings chain outward by compass geography from Edaria's four gates: **north → North Meadow → North Wood**, **west → West Meadow → the Swamp/the Graveyard**, **south → South Meadow → the Wastes → the Beach**, **east → Autumn Wood** (and on toward Troll Village). Of these, the North Meadow, North Wood, West and South Meadows, and Autumn Wood are in the world. No border and no door carries a rank or access check — a dreamer may walk anywhere the ground goes; the nightmares are the barrier.

**The near meadows** (the gentle ring, hugging Edaria — *Tier 1*)
- **North Meadow / West Meadow / South Meadow / South-West Meadow** — open grassland, the first ground a dreamer walks. Faint, almost harmless nightmares drift here.

**The forests** (the second ring — *Tier 2*)
- **North Wood** — dark pine forest north past the North Meadow.
- **Autumn Wood** — a great autumn-colored wood east of Edaria, the largest near region.
- **South-West Wood** — a long forest strip running south, toward Griswal's land.

**The mire & the dead** (west, where the dream rots — *Tier 3*)
- **The Swamp** — a wide swamp of pools and reeds, with a **lake** and a **cave** sunk beneath it. The dream waterlogged and sour.
- **Church & Graveyard** — a church and graveyard beyond the West Meadow, a *thin place* where the dead don't rest and nightmares pool thickest. Beneath it lies the **crypt**, Somnio's first lair. *(being built)*

**The wilds** (organized danger — *Tier 4*)
- **Troll Village** — a troll settlement east beyond the Autumn Wood; a warband of nightmares with a camp of their own.
- **Rest Cave** — a cave on the eastern road.
- **Griswal's & the Bear Cave** — a lone homestead in the southwest and a bear-cave beside it.
- **The Jungle** — dense jungle far to the east.

**The frayed edge** (the rim, nearest the unraveling — *Tier 5*)
- **The Wastes** — barrens south of Edaria where the dream has worn through to bare nothing.
- **Savanna → Desert** — savanna giving way to open desert, the hot dead far-east.
- **The Beach** — the southern coast; the sea begins here.
- **Pirate Shore** — a pirate shore at the far south; raiders washed up from beyond the dream's edge.

## People of Edaria

- **Libus** — the gatekeeper-librarian. The first face every dreamer sees; keeper of the threshold, fond of rules, secretly fond of newcomers, and the giver of a newcomer's first errand *(being built)*. (Edaria's old souls carry dream-Latin names — Libus from *liber*, and the rest below likewise.)
- **Pugnax, the Fighter master** — master of the Fighters' hall (*pugnax*, combative); blunt, scarred, teaches by sparring.
- **Sana, the Healer master** (*sanare*, to heal) — mistress of the Healers' hall; keeper of the Moonstone lore, gentle and exacting.
- **Tacita, the Mystic master** (*tacita*, the silent) — barely seen; teaches the secretive craft, answers questions with questions, and sets her trial in hints. She keeps the tower that opens only at night.
- **Mercus, the shopkeeper** (*merx*, wares) — deals in gear, from the plain pieces he sells a newcomer to whatever a seasoned dreamer brings back from the edge.
- **Quieta, the innkeeper** (*quies*, rest) — keeps the wake-point; knows every regular by their falling.
- **The priest at the church** — tends the thin place by the graveyard; the source of a dreamer's first real errands, as the dead grow restless, down into the crypt. *(being built)*
- **Griswal** — an old hermit in the southwest, longest-dreaming of them all. A lore-keeper, and a master for those who venture past the gentle rings.

## The nightmares — a bestiary by tier

Monsters are the dream fraying, and they grow with distance from Edaria — the small→big progression the world is built around. Each should read as a *leaked piece of dread*, sharing a coherent visual language distinct from the dreamers. The tiers track the gazetteer:

- **Tier 1 — the Shallows** (Arena, near meadows): the **Ghost** a dreamer first meets in the Arena; faint wisps and drifting dreads.
- **Tier 2 — the Forests**: beasts the dream shaped wrong — wolves, boars, a forest bogey.
- **Tier 3 — the Mire & the Dead** (the Swamp, the Graveyard): swamp-things (a giant ant, a swamp-mushroom horror) and restless undead — skeleton warriors and their minions, stronger Ghosts where the dead won't lie still, and the **Necromancer** in the crypt *(being built)*.
- **Tier 4 — the Wilds** (Troll Village, caves, jungle): bigger, organized threats — **trolls**, a cave **bear**, jungle predators.
- **Tier 5 — the Frayed Edge** (the Wastes, the Desert, Pirate Shore): the harshest — desert horrors, **pirates** raiding from beyond the rim, and whatever leaks straight from the waking void where the dream gives out.

Any kind may come as an **elite**, and a lair ends at a boss. *(being built)*
